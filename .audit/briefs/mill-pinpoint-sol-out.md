# Sol pinpoint ruling

Consult reconciled through `.audit/mill-sweep21.json` and the level-cache
manifest. F18-CONTORACLE reproduced both frozen input gates and sweep 19's P3
entries exactly. Its frozen `SPEC` defines a pricing map with no fitting, no
pass letters, and no verdict column. This page reports its measurements as
facts and derives no F18 letter or verdict.

## A. The pinpoint

### Root cause

The mill is trying to solve a two-sided collision while observing only the
impulse side.

At one-minute grain, the 16-feature flow/state plane measures the strength of a
session displacement impulse. G1 then treats each local zigzag extreme crossed
by that impulse as if it were an independent support-or-resistance decision.
Most are not barriers. They are porous waypoints in the same auction path. A
hold or break is determined by **barrier strength relative to impulse strength**,
but the tested candidate formation supplies the impulse and repeatedly aliases
its path while supplying almost no causal level memory or location for the
barrier. Formation does not identify which repeated encounter is the real
collision. In the continuation frame it also fixes the occurrence side. The
plane therefore cannot tell which episode to spend or whether price will reject
the level or continue through it.

That is one market mechanism, not nine post-hoc explanations. It also separates
two questions that were being conflated. The existing universe contains enough
hindsight cash. Its problem is not missing profitable prices. Its problem is
that the learning rows are repeated, weakly formed claims that an arbitrary
local extreme is a barrier. The input plane can estimate how much movement is
coming, but not whether the particular price in front of it has enough prior
defence to stop that movement. Sweep 21 localizes the omission. Hindsight bar
choice on the first episode still misses the registered joint route. Keeping
the same side but adding hindsight choice of episode within each cell-side slot
clears all three raw frame rungs. The binding missing bit inside the repeated
rows is episode identity.
Side choice adds another rich oracle, but it is not needed to establish frame
capacity. Exact placement is secondary to causal formation at a meaningful
level.

The strongest direct evidence is the conjunction, not any one statistic. Sweep
15 says the information is magnitude rather than sign and that large outcomes
cluster. Sweep 17 says more predicted impulse means less holding. Sweep 19 says
observing an actual retest adds timing information but that the extreme remains
porous. Sweep 20 says magnitude-selected rows have more favorable and adverse
excursion while a coin side under ATR stops is still negative. Sweep 21 says
touch beats later confirmation, first-episode oracle timing is insufficient,
and same-side episode choice restores hindsight capacity. These are the
expected signatures of an observed impulse colliding with an unmeasured
barrier.

### Falsifier

Form first-approach events at causally known, previously defended price zones.
On chronological folds, add strictly prior level memory and location to the
same impulse estimate. The mechanism is falsified if a powered test finds both
of the following on NKD and SI:

1. the 95 percent upper bound on the out-of-fold improvement in hold-or-break
   discrimination over the impulse-only baseline is at or below zero; and
2. the 95 percent upper bound on exact seated cash improvement over a
   count-, phase-, time-, and magnitude-matched non-level approach is at or
   below zero.

An underpowered result is UNRESOLVED, not a falsifier. A stable current-G1
flow-only policy that clears both rungs with lawful seating would also falsify
the stronger claim that barrier-aware formation is necessary.

Sweep 20 tested the impulse half and did not falsify it. It found real magnitude
and excursion separation, then killed standalone symmetric magnitude-asymmetry
monetization. Sweep 21 did not read the missing barrier term, so it cannot test
the direct falsifier. It instead closes the placement-only explanation. P2
beats P3 by 72.1 to 141.3 USD per asset-day and P4 by 97.4 to 149.4 on matched
episodes. Every such contrast survives the 18-cell max-stat. P2 itself is
negative on both deciding assets under both labels. The best causal line is
only 153.8 USD per day on SI, while NKD is negative.

The first-episode P5 line reaches 1094.1 on NKD and 1653.9 on SI under the
1800-second label. Under the close label it reaches 320.7 and 1194.9. It does
not carry both deciding rungs. The registered P5 per-cell-side frame then
spends one more hindsight bit to choose the episode while keeping the same side. It
reaches 4108.5 on NKD and 4726.3 on SI under the 1800-second label, and 4238.5
and 5102.0 under the close label. P6 also clears both deciding rungs after it
frees bar and side. It flips the occurrence side on 46.6 to 63.0 percent of
rows. These are unseated pricing facts, not an F18 verdict or a causal pass.
They show that exact bar choice is not enough, while episode identity is worth
more than the target inside the same-side frame. The landed 24-column level
cache enables the direct falsifier. Its existence is not evidence that the
falsifier will pass.

## B. The candidate answer

**No, candidate availability is not the problem. Yes, a differently formed
candidate universe should materially raise the probability of a causal pass.
More candidates from the present generator should not.**

This does not reverse the earlier generator ruling. The current universe has
2564 / 3670 / 4434 USD per asset-day of hindsight cash, and the coarse view
keeps about 93 percent of the ceiling with roughly fourteen times fewer rows.
Availability is ample. Formation is poor for causal learning because it labels
many aliases of one displacement as separate barrier decisions. Sweep 21 makes
the distinction sharper. The first P5 episode with hindsight bar choice does
not carry the joint route. The P5 per-cell-side frame clears it after spending
the extra hindsight bit that chooses which repeated episode matters, without
changing side. Different formation is useful because it can make episode
identity and level state causal, not because the current tape lacks paying
prices.

### Formation rule

Name the rule **LEVELCOLLISION**.

1. Before a touch, define a price zone from information already known at the
   prior completed one-minute bar. Eligible zones are prior-day high, low, or
   close; the cached prior-EXPLORE value edges; or a same-day price zone with
   strictly earlier resolved touch, hold, and break history.
2. Form one candidate on the first approach from outside a fold-trained
   ATR-normalized outer band. Deduplicate by `(asset, day, phase, level,
   approach side)` until price has causally departed or breached the zone.
3. Arm a fade-side resting limit at a fold-trained depth inside the zone and
   cancel it after a fold-trained duration. Raw ticks may decide crossings and
   fills, but no subminute value becomes an input.
4. Estimate impulse from the frozen out-of-fold magnitude channel. Estimate
   barrier strength from level memory and location. Before dispatch, freeze the
   exact monotone rule as a fold-trained threshold on barrier score relative to
   scaled impulse score. Admit only strong-barrier fades. Skip weak-barrier
   collisions. Do not search model families after outcomes are visible.
5. Freeze exactly one post-touch companion at P2, the retest touch. P2 beats P3
   and P4 on every asset and both labels after max-stat adjustment. Report it
   separately from the pre-touch line. Do not carry P3, P4, or P7 forward.
   P2's negative absolute cash means that F18 does not promote it. F19 must earn
   selection value from level formation under its own frozen letters.

The cache's `ps_*` fields and value edges refer to the previous **EXPLORE**
session, not necessarily the immediately previous market session. The test
must label them that way. Immediate prior-day high, low, and close remain a
separate lawful location family. See `.audit/mill-levels-build.json` and
`tools/mill/levels.py`.

### One decisive priced test

Run **F19-LEVELCOLLISION** once on EXPLORE. Its input plane is the coarse
one-minute mill plane, the landed level cache, frozen out-of-fold magnitude,
and raw suffixes only for fills, crossings, and the frozen outcome law. Fit
thresholds by prior days separately for asset and phase. Price the causal rule
in exact chronology under one contract, one position per asset, twelve entries
per portfolio day, all binding MDD ledgers, costs, and the existing outcome
law.

The controls are fixed before the run. Compare each selected event with a
same-asset, day, phase, approach-time, and magnitude-bin G1 event. For the
level-specific null, freeze the fitted rule and block-permute level memory and
location among scoring events in the same asset, phase, and magnitude bin while
holding count and time fixed. Also report the best lawfully priced event within
each formed LEVELCOLLISION opportunity as an exploratory ceiling. Max-stat
covers the pre-touch line, the single P2 companion, and their frozen controls.
F19 has no broader placement grid.

Pre-registered letters are:

- `LEVELCOLLISION-LIVE` only if NKD and SI each exceed 1500 USD per asset-day
  at the point estimate and mean minus two standard errors, every binding MDD
  is below 1000 USD, the cap and occupancy laws hold, the paired control
  survives max-stat at 0.05, and adjacent fold-trained thresholds do not flip
  the result.
- `LEVELCOLLISION-UNRESOLVED` if the formed ceiling can carry both rungs and
  the causal matched delta is positive, but power or one live bound fails.
- `LEVELCOLLISION-KILL` if the formed ceiling misses either deciding rung, or
  if a powered deciding asset has a non-positive 95 percent upper bound against
  its matched control. This kills this formation rule, not the goal.

### Lawful successor and the bar for changing law

If LEVELCOLLISION is KILL, the next lawful lever is
**F20-STRUCTBREAK-PULLBACK**. Form one candidate only after a one-minute close
causally breaches a prior persistent level, then arm the first pullback from
the other side. It uses the same level cache and magnitude plane but asks a
porous level to confirm continuation instead of asking it to hold. Pre-register
`STRUCTBREAK-LIVE`, `STRUCTBREAK-UNRESOLVED`, and `STRUCTBREAK-KILL` under the
same replay, control, rung, cap, and MDD bounds. This remains at lawful
one-minute grain and needs no user exception.

`STRUCTBREAK-KILL` routes to `F21-SUBMINUTE-ORDER-ORACLE`, an outcome-only raw
tick map with no promotion authority. It tests whether within-minute ordering
has enough capacity to justify asking the USER to revisit the grain law.

Do not ask to relax the grain law from sweep 18 alone. A request for subminute
inputs would be justified only if an outcome-only raw-tick oracle shows on both
NKD and SI that within-minute touch, absorption, and rejection ordering can
clear the rungs and MDD, while powered one-minute LEVELCOLLISION and
STRUCTBREAK tests have non-positive upper bounds because those orderings
collapse into the same bar. Until that evidence exists, the next lever is
formation at the current lawful grain.
