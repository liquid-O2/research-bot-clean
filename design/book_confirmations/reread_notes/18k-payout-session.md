## Mechanism used at the licensed entries: refresh consistency (page 11)

Not a fifth trade. This is the check that separated Trade 3 from Trades 1 and 2.

SETUP: a resting order at a level of interest is being hit.

ORDERED checks:
1. Hit, then the same size reappears at the same price. That is one refresh. On its own it only says somebody wants the price defended.
2. Read the second and third refresh. Real defense: hit, replaced, hit, replaced, steady pace, steady size.
3. Bluff: each refresh a little smaller, pacing stretching out, then it stops coming back. Thinning precedes the level giving way.
4. Trade 3's tell, restated here: "not that sellers showed up once, but that they kept showing up with the same conviction on the retest."

Wait: 2-3 refresh cycles. Seconds to a couple of minutes after the first test. No figure on this page. The live quotes are from the same session's tape.

TRIGGER: with the refreshing side, or against the thinning side. INVALIDATION: a single absorption print. "Absorption only says an aggressive order got stopped passively once. Consistency across several refreshes is what tells you whether that was a wall or a coincidence."

PASSED on: acting on one absorption print.

VERBATIM (page 11): "Sellers common, they're refreshing pretty strongly."
VERBATIM (page 11): "these buyers are failing, they're refilling again, the refill concept with selling pressure coming in with aggressiveness."
VERBATIM (page 11): "a single absorption print is never enough to act on by itself."

FEATURE MAPPING: CONF-REPLENISH, CONF-REFILL-VS-PRINTS. disc_evt_reloads + disc_quote_rebuild. Named gap: the tell is the *trend across refreshes* of size and interval, not a per-second reload count. That is G4. G3 if "digits" thinning is how they see the size decay. No DOM is drawn in this PDF, so the session's own figures do not show the ladder they are talking about.

## Distinction used in the read: covering vs dealer hedging (page 12)

Not an entry. Two different mechanisms that both print as buying.

1. Options dealer hedging. Short-gamma desk buying into strength and selling into weakness to stay flat. Amplifies whatever is already happening. This is the page 4 gamma comment. It is not a who-won-the-price read.
2. Ordinary short covering. "buyers are covering themselves, here are these lows." Underwater shorts buying back. Shows up as passive buying at a level already tested and failed to break. That is what this framework means by a protected high.

PASSED on: treating dealer hedging as evidence that buyers are winning.

FEATURE MAPPING: covering-versus-hedging attribution is UNKNOWN. No participant flag in the stream. Nearest proxy: passive buying at a level already tested-and-failed-to-break, i.e. disc_level_z* hold + opposite-side disc_evt_* without a break.

VERBATIM (page 12): "buyers are covering themselves, here are these lows."
VERBATIM (page 12): "Both show up as buying pressure. Only one of them tells you anything about who is actually winning the fight for the price."
