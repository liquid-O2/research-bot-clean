## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists, written before the open: areas where reactions are likely on **both** sides. The thesis names what price has done, what it is doing, and why. It does not name a direction.
2. Ask: is price **at** a marked area, not near one, not a different one that looks similar. Something has to be holding: aggression arriving and the level not giving way.
3. Ask: where the idea is wrong. That is the other side of the holders, just past the shelf. Control is whoever is actually holding, not the thesis's overall objective.
4. Wait. Getting stopped is not a reason to get back in, and not a reason to stay out. Re-entry is live **only** if price comes back to the same level. If it does not return, the loss stands.
5. Only then: entry / wait / pass. Ratio is checked after the levels exist, not chosen first. Size, daily dollar target, and max-loss caps on p11 are out of scope.

The session's 44% win rate, $500 close, and R:R boxes are management arithmetic. The entry craft on these pages is: thesis without a bias, hold at a marked shelf, re-enter only on a return to that shelf.

## Page 3 glossary (no market figure)

Terms used the same way through the session. Entry-relevant:

- **AGGRESSION**: market orders hitting the book. Someone paying up to get filled rather than waiting.
- **ABSORPTION**: aggression arriving at a level and the level not moving. Passive size is taking the other side.
- **REFILL**: resting size reappearing at a level after being eaten. The wall is being rebuilt rather than pulled.
- **MINOR HIGH VOLUME NODE**: a shelf inside the profile where trade repeatedly happened. Reactions cluster there.
- **FAILED AUCTION**: price leaves a range, fails to find acceptance, and comes back inside. The failure is the signal.
- **PROTECTED HIGH OR LOW**: a swing the market has already defended once. (He uses it as a trailing-stop reference. Trail/stop management is out of scope. The fact of a defended swing is still an entry location.)
- **SPEED OF TAPE**: how fast trades are printing. A jump in speed at a level says the level is being contested.
- **CUMULATIVE DELTA**: running difference between buying and selling aggression. Divergence from price matters more than the level.
- **SQUEEZE**: aggression building against a level that will not give. It either breaks, or it fails and unwinds hard.
- **ORIGIN OF THE MOVE**: the exact point where control changed hands. It is the reference the rest of the move is measured from.

DEALING RANGE: the band price is currently rotating inside, bounded by where it last failed at each end. LONG GAMMA REGIME: dealer positioning that dampens moves. Context, not an entry trigger.

## Page 10 checklist (entry-side)

BEFORE THE SESSION
- Thesis written. What price has done, what it is doing, and why. No direction stated.
- Areas marked. Where reactions are likely on both sides, not just the side you prefer.
- Objective named. The higher timeframe level that is actually owed something.
- Failure named. What would have to happen for the objective to be wrong.

BEFORE THE ENTRY
- Price is at a marked area. Not near one, and not a different one that looks similar.
- Something is holding. Aggression arriving and the level not giving way.
- Stop location decided first. Just past the level, where the reason for the trade is gone.
- Ratio checked, not chosen. The objective is where it is. If the ratio is poor, skip it.

IN THE TRADE (management, out of scope as a goal path; listed so it is not imported)
- Move to break even when the reason weakens. Not at a fixed number of ticks.
- Trail behind protected levels. Swings the market has already defended once.
- Cut fast when wrong.

AFTER
- Re-entry only if price returns to the level. Otherwise the trade is finished.
- Daily objective reached, stop. (Dollar daily target is out of scope.)

## What we implemented wrong

We put a direction in the thesis and then treated every failed level as an argument to try again. This PDF writes areas of reaction and a named failure, then waits for price to return to the **same** shelf. Spike-outs are not a new signal and not a reason to chase. Gap G1 still applies: without delta-by-price you cannot see "buyers holding" as a row. The re-entry rule is a wait, not a score.
