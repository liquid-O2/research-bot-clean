Ethos Order Flow, Sires x Ethos. Case study of one NQ trader (Keani / Alive). All 33 pages read as images.

This PDF is pass-rules. The average unprofitable trader fires the order-flow trigger before the auction has confirmed the same story from more than one angle. Waiting is lawful. Size, exits, minis, and neural are out of scope; they appear on the boards and are noted only so they are not mistaken for entry gates.

## Sequence (not a score bag)

Ordered filter. Any fail is PASS / no-trade. Do not average these into a confluence score. Page 13's whole point is that stacking a fourth unmeasured confluence is how the account gets worse.

1. **Inside the box.** The candidate is one of the named features the framework permits: absorption, exhaustion, trapped buyers or sellers, aggression, a level, on daily-VP shelves. Outside the box (trend lines, generic S/R, footprint noise, a mid-session switch of tools) is not a trade. (pp 9–10, 27)
2. **Daily thesis already written.** Auction location, who should be in control, logical destination. Thesis is a filter, not a Discord opinion. No thesis, no candidate. (pp 17, 19, 27, 29 cards 02–03)
3. **Thesis gate.** This trade agrees with that location. A short at the lower boundary of the curve / VAL is an automatic skip: buyers should step in at a discount. (pp 19, 25, 27)
4. **Name the setup as a pair.** Feature known *and* regime known (fade or continuation). Features are regime-specific: absorption suits fading a trend; trapped sellers pile up at the bottom of continuation. If either name is missing, wait. (pp 19, 27, 29 card 06, 30)
5. **Open-above-value long, if that is the day.** Current TPO opens fully above yesterday's value, A period sitting clear of prior VAH. Then wait until around 10:00 for day type to establish from profile shape. Then, in this order only: value building higher (buyers in control, sellers exhausting) → rejection off POC or prior VAH → current VAH breaks on aggressive buying imbalances → price comes back to those imbalances → the buyers who broke defend the retest. If they fold, you have the answer and it cost nothing. (pp 21–22)
6. **DOM last, and in fixed order.** Location (who should be in control *here*, already decided by the thesis), then pacing (is effort being paid, or is price rotating in the same few ticks), then digits (who is thinning, who is building). Entry is when pacing then upticks: effort finally getting its reward. Digits-first or imbalance-first is the beginner tell. (pp 25–26, 28 diamond, 29 card 08)
7. **Absorption extra gate.** If the named feature is absorption, require 3-tick aggression toward the absorbed side *before* entry. Author's counted sample: absorption fails 27% of the time without that print. (pp 24, 28, 29 footer)
8. **Destination exists.** A logical auction level to go to (POC, value, VAH, poor high or low). Price seeks value 60–80% of the time. A clean break-and-retest into nothing is a clean entry into a losing trade. Thesis first, always. The entry is the last gate, never the first. (pp 21, 25, 28, 30)
9. **If any box is unticked, no trade yet. Waiting is free.** (p 30 footer)

Confluence count is not a step. Three vs four "confluences" is the failed score bag. You cannot know whether the stricter filter helps until you have collected the 20 trades, and Keani had collected zero. (pp 13–14)

## Failed sequences (entered too soon)

Each row is what the average unprofitable trader did, then the ordered wait they skipped.

### F1. Assumption entry (p 7)

- **Too soon.** Best winner of the week was a short during a Trump speech: "I just assumed that we would keep going down." It paid. He called it luck.
- **Wait for.** Thesis location → named feature and regime → auction confirmation from more than one angle. An assumption is not a confirmation.
- **Mapping.** Named gap: we have no "assumption vs confirmed sequence" bit. Closest is "candidate formed with no disc_level_z* defense and no disc_evt_* aggression at a named level." Do not treat a speech/news impulse as a feature.

### F2. Trigger-first / thesis-last (pp 21, 25, 27)

- **Too soon.** Came to the DOM or footprint looking for a magic entry trigger. Fired the imbalance, the absorption, or the break-retest, then asked where we were.
- **Wait for.** (1) auction location / who should be in control, (2) named setup, (3) then DOM Location → Pacing → Digits.
- **Quote (p 25).** "If you don't understand where we are in the auction, you'll never be truly profitable on the DOM, on the footprint, on any other order-flow entry trigger." 38:35
- **Mapping.** disc_auction_* / disc_prior_* must pass before disc_evt_* or disc_quote_*. Scoring tape events without location is this failure.

### F3. Short at the discount (pp 19, 25, 27)

- **Too soon.** Holding a short at the lower boundary of the volume profile because the DOM still showed sellers.
- **Wait for.** Location first: at VAL, price is at a discount, buyers should step in, price seeks POC/value 60–80% of the time. Give buyers leniency on the DOM. Hold sellers to a higher standard. Skip the short.
- **Figure-only (p 27).** Left of the Thesis-filter diamond, grey text: "A short at the lower boundary of the curve? Buyers should step in. Skip it." Dashed arrow from a failed thesis gate goes to a pill "No trade. Wait."
- **Mapping.** disc_auction_* location vs VAL/POC. A short candidate at the session low-volume-profile edge is a hard PASS unless the thesis already said sellers are in control *and* value is building lower.

### F4. Open-above-value chase (pp 21–22)

- **Too soon.** Saw TPO open above yesterday's value and bought the open, or bought the first break of current VAH, or bought the first touch of VAH without a defended retest.
- **Wait for, ordered.** A period fully above prior VAH → ~10:00 day type from profile shape → value building higher → rejection at POC or prior VAH → aggressive buying imbalances *doing the breaking* of current VAH → retest of those imbalances → defense by the breaking buyers. DOM at the retest still agrees. HTF objective exists.
- **Figure-only (p 22).** Blackboard: tall vertical on the left (the open sitting clear of prior value), a circle on the rejection, horizontals labeled VAH and POC, break arrow up through VAH, a second circle on the hold of that break from above. The entry mark is on the retest circle, not on the vertical open, not on the first break tick.
- **Mapping.** disc_prior_* (prior VAH/value), session-open vs prior VA, disc_level_z* at POC then at the *break-imbalance rows* (not generic VAH). Named gaps: TPO A-period vs prior VA as a binary; TPO day-type from profile shape around 10:00; "the specific imbalance rows that broke VAH" as the retest level.

### F5. Bare break-retest into nothing (p 21)

- **Too soon.** Clean break-and-retest, no higher-timeframe objective, nothing for the auction to go to.
- **Wait for.** Thesis names a destination (POC, value, VAH, poor high or low) *before* the trigger. Time of day still applies. DOM at the retest still applies.
- **Quote (p 21).** "A clean break-and-retest into nothing is a clean entry into a losing trade. Thesis first, always. The entry is the last gate, never the first."
- **Mapping.** Candidate must carry a destination level from disc_auction_* / disc_prior_* / poor-high-low. Named gap: poor highs and poor lows from the 30-min TPO as first-class levels.

### F6. Absorption without 3-tick aggression (pp 24, 28, 29)

- **Too soon.** Saw absorption and entered.
- **Wait for.** Named as absorption *and* fade regime. Location agrees. Then 3-tick aggression toward the absorbed side. Then DOM Location → Pacing → Digits.
- **Figure-only (p 24).** Same board fragment as p 16 left: "abs , +VAH" at top. Circled "27%." with a left arrow. Circled "3 tick" with a down arrow into it. Caption: absorption fails 27% of the time unless there is 3-tick aggression toward the absorbed side.
- **Mapping.** Absorption event (disc_evt_* / disc_level_z*) is setup, not trigger. Trigger is a 3-tick aggressive displacement toward the absorbed side. Named gap: "3-tick aggression toward absorbed side" as a confirmation *after* absorption, not concurrent. 27% is the author's sample on his markets, not a universal number.

### F7. Imbalance while price is rotating (p 26)

- **Too soon.** Huge divergence of buyers over sellers, treated the imbalance as the entry.
- **Wait for.** Pacing first: if that effort is happening while price stagnates, rotating in the same few ticks, it is unpaid effort. Warning, whatever the imbalance says. Entry only when pacing then upticks.
- **Figure-only (p 26).** Right of the 1-2-3 stack: "Effort vs R" with circling arrows, then a jagged sideways scribble (price rotating in a tight range). The zigzag *is* the pass condition. Caption names it: "Pacing (is effort being rewarded, or is price just rotating; the zigzag at right)."
- **Mapping.** Named gap: effort/reward while price is confined to a few ticks (imbalance or DOM size without displacement). Closest: w{15..1800}_* flow vs displacement. A large disc_evt_* count with no price progress is a PASS, not a plus.

### F8. Digits-first DOM (pp 25–26)

- **Too soon.** Read bid-ask digit size at the open and traded the thinning.
- **Wait for.** Location (not on the DOM), then pacing, then digits. Digits: sellers refreshing from two- and three-digit sizes down to singles = thinning / weakness. Buyers stepping from two and three digits to four and five = building effort. Those reads license nothing until location and pacing already passed.
- **Figure-only (p 26).** Left stack, circled 1 Location, 2 Pacing, 3 Dig, arrows down the list. Order is drawn, not described as a bag.
- **Mapping.** Named gap: digit-length of displayed DOM size (2–3 digit → 1 digit thinning; 2–3 → 4–5 building). disc_quote_* depletion/rebuild is the nearest family and is not the same observable.

### F9. Confluence bag (pp 13–14)

- **Too soon.** Four confluences, therefore better, therefore take it (and risk more).
- **Wait for.** Nothing. Do not add a confluence as a pass rule until 20 trades of the three-feature version exist to compare. The stricter filter can produce a worse account by cutting frequency. Unmeasured stacking is the failure, not the fourth feature itself.
- **Figure-only (p 14).** "20 = 70%. 1:5RR" with a down arrow to 65 then 60. Two overlapping circles: A marked 3 and 150, B marked 4 and 200. Arrows in and out. 73 at the right. Caption: one extra confluence, double the risk, zero evidence it wins more.
- **Mapping.** Do not implement "confluence_count >= N". That is the score bag this PDF exists to kill.

### F10. Mid-session tool switch (p 9)

- **Too soon.** Trading daily-VP shelves off the DOM, then randomly switching to footprint imbalances mid-session. Feels like flexibility. It is noise once the box exists.
- **Wait for.** Stay inside the boxed features for the session. New tool = new feature, which needs its own 10–20 sample, not a live hop.
- **Mapping.** A regime or tool change mid-session without a named setup is a PASS.

### F11. Optimising the entry first (pp 17–18)

- **Too soon.** The beginner start: better trigger, better DOM read, before any tagged sample exists.
- **Wait for.** Data collection, then product literacy, then the real diagnosis. Entry optimisation is the last of the four questions, and only then as a 20-trade A/B.
- **Figure-only (p 18).** Board pre-written before the interview: "Do you collect data? Do you understand how a Prop firm works? What is your main issue is pre main issue. Have you optimised your entries etc." Entries last on the board, on purpose.

### F12. Checklist skip (p 30)

- **Too soon.** Any unticked box and the trade is still taken.
- **Wait for.** All per-trade boxes. Footer: "If one is unticked, the answer is no trade yet. Waiting is free."

### p 19. The data-collection system (no chart)

Two kinds of data: quantitative dashboard numbers, and qualitative record of what you saw and why you acted. Beginners need qualitative first, because it tells you what to count.

Daily thesis first: where are we in the higher-timeframe auction, and who should be in control? Holding a short at the lower boundary of the curve? Possibly not. Buyers step in at a discount.

Then the **feature set**. Thesis is a feature, entry is a feature; deeper, absorption, trapped buyers and sellers, aggression. Each is testable. Collect 10 to 20 trades per feature. Absorption against trapped traders, level one against level two, drop the loser. Write the regime every time, fade or continuation.

Keani asked if his p-day and b-day rule was "good." Refused. Collect 20 to 30 of your own; if it made no difference, it is noise, drop it. Do not encode p-day/b-day as a pass rule.

**Quotes.** "Thesis is extremely important, to filter your trades. As a filter." 22:08. "Why are you asking me for a subjective judgment? You need to actually look at the day for yourself." 25:18

### p 21. Worked example: open above value (the load-bearing sequence)

Keani's own setup, vagueness squeezed out.

His words: current day's TPO opens fully above yesterday's value, A period sitting clear of prior VAH. Waits until around 10:00 for day type to establish from the shape of the profile, then watches current VAH for a break and a retest, entering off the volume profile.

Sires's ordered AMT read (follow in order):

1. Value opening and building higher = buyers in control, sellers getting exhausted.
2. Price will probably reject off the POC or the previous day's VAH.
3. Once above it, current VAH breaks. You should see aggressive buying imbalances doing the breaking.
4. The test that decides everything: price comes back to those imbalances. If the buyers who broke the level defend them, that retest is a good long. If they fold, you have your answer, and it cost nothing.

What made it good: **every step is the auction confirming the same story from a different angle: value shift, rejection, aggressive break, defended retest.** No step is decoration.

Caveats, load-bearing: still subject to time of day, still subject to what the DOM shows at the retest, still needs a decent higher-timeframe objective. Thesis first. Entry last.

### p 23. How to journal (protocol, not a trigger)

Record the whole session. Scrub to the moment *just before* each entry. Write what you were thinking then. After-the-close screenshots grade the trade by an outcome already decided. Keani had been doing exactly that.

**Quotes.** "What was I thinking before I entered the trade? That is the most important information you'll have." 29:33. ### p 25. The DOM: Location, Pacing, Digits (part 1)

Beginners come to the DOM looking for a magic entry trigger. There isn't one. Three reads, fixed order, and the first one isn't even on the DOM.

**One: Location.** Before you read a single order, know where you are in the auction. At the lower boundary of the volume profile, price is at a discount and buyers should step in; price seeks the POC and value 60 to 80% of the time. Give buyers leniency on the DOM. Hold sellers to a higher standard. Decide who gets the benefit of the doubt *before* the tape starts arguing.

**Quote.** "If you don't understand where we are in the auction, you'll never be truly profitable on the DOM, on the footprint, on any other order-flow entry trigger." 38:35

### p 26. Pacing, then digits

**Two: Pacing.** Effort against reward. A huge divergence of buyers over sellers while price stagnates, rotating in the same few ticks, is massive effort with no reward, and effort that isn't being paid is a warning, whatever the imbalance says.

**Three: Digits.** At the open you can't compute bid-ask maths fast enough, so read digit size. Sellers refreshing from two and three-digit sizes down to singles are thinning: weakness. Buyers stepping from two and three digits to four and five are building effort. When the pacing then upticks, effort finally getting its reward, that's the entry.

Location, then pacing, then digits, always in that order, always after the thesis. Only your own DOM recordings of your own entries are worth studying.

**Figure-only.** "Dom" header. Circled 1 Location, 2 Pacing, 3 Dig, arrows down. Right: "Effort vs R" with rotation arrows and the sideways zigzag that is the unpaid-effort pass. Caption: Location (who should be in control here), Pacing (is effort being rewarded, or is price just rotating), Digits (who is thinning, who is building). In that order, always after the thesis.

## Feature mapping (best-effort) and named gaps

Computable with existing families, as a filter not a score:

- Auction location vs VAL/POC/VAH, prior value: disc_auction_*, disc_prior_*, disc_ib_*.
- Defense / fold at a named level (POC rejection, VAH retest, imbalance-row retest): disc_level_z*.
- Aggressive buying doing a break: disc_evt_* (attacks/lifts), w{15..1800}_* displacement.
- Tape speed / time-of-day 10:00 wait: disc_tclock (the clock only, not day type).
- Quote depletion / rebuild as a weak cousin of "thinning": disc_quote_*.

Named gaps (deliverables, not failures):

- **G-thesis-gate.** Daily written thesis as a hard gate. No per-second family is "who should be in control today."
- **G-A-period-clear.** TPO A period sitting fully above prior VAH.
- **G-day-type-10.** Day type established from 30-min profile shape around 10:00. A clock check is not this.
- **G-break-imbalance-rows.** The specific buying-imbalance prices that broke current VAH become the retest level. Generic VAH is not that level.
- **G-3tick-absorbed.** 3-tick aggression toward the absorbed side *after* absorption. 27% is the author's sample.
- **G-pacing-unpaid.** Large effort (imbalance or size) while price rotates in the same few ticks. Warning / PASS, not a plus.
- **G-dom-digits.** Digit-length of DOM size (2–3 → 1 thinning; 2–3 → 4–5 building). disc_quote_* is not digit length.
- **G-feature-regime-pair.** Feature {absorption, trapped, aggression, level} AND regime {fade, continuation} both named, and they must fit (absorption for fade, trapped sellers at continuation lows). Separate scores will re-create F9.
- **G-poor-highs-lows.** 30-min TPO poor highs, poor lows, single prints as destination/invalidation levels.
- **G-pday-bday.** Keani's rule, explicitly not blessed. Needs a 20–30 sample before it is anything.

Do not build confluence_count. That is F9.
