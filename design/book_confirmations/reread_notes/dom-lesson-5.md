## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists. Never watch the DOM in a vacuum. Come to it with the shelf, the ledge, or the value edge already marked.
2. Ask: is the level actually being fought. Reset is implied later in lesson 6. Here: watch who is aggressive at the level, and what the passive side does (hold, reload and stack, or pull).
3. Ask: who owns Delta at that price. Delta is the running score, net market buys minus market sells **at each price level**. That is G1 on the ladder. Heavy positive delta while price goes nowhere means someone is absorbing the buying.
4. Wait for the conversation, not a single print. Speed of tape is the pulse. A tape that rips at a level is urgency. A tape that freezes is hesitation or a big passive order soaking everything up. Spread widening is liquidity pulling: do not lean on the level, do not chase into the gap.
5. Only then: the ladder is the trigger for a level you already have. Aggression with movement is continuation. Aggression without movement is absorption. Pass if the DOM is being watched as a location-finder. It never invents the level.

## Page 4 (no figure)

Every participant on the ladder is one of two kinds.

AGGRESSIVE, MARKET ORDERS. They cross the spread and hit the ask or the bid because they want the fill immediately. Best ask 5000.25, best bid 5000.00, an aggressive buyer clicks buy market and instantly pays 5000.25, they are lifting the offer. Aggression is urgency and conviction, this is what actually moves price.

PASSIVE, LIMIT ORDERS. They place a bid or an offer and wait. A passive buyer rests a limit at 4999.75 and waits for sellers to come to him. Passive because they never chase. They provide the liquidity the aggressive side consumes.

THE INTERACTION IS THE MARKET. Price moves when aggression eats through passivity. Every level that holds or breaks, every wick, every trend leg is that one contest, repeated.

## Page 6 (no ladder figure)

SPEED OF TAPE. How fast the time and sales prints scroll. A tape that suddenly rips at a level is urgency arriving. A tape that freezes at a level is hesitation, or a big passive order soaking everything up. The footprint is the history, the speed is the pulse.

THE SPREAD. The gap between best bid and best ask. One tick when liquidity is deep and healthy. Widens the instant liquidity thins out, which is your earliest warning that a level is about to break or a fast move is coming.

FAST TAPE INTO A LEVEL. Aggression is committed. If the level still holds, that is absorption worth trading. If it breaks, the speed carries the continuation.

SPREAD WIDENS. Liquidity is pulling. Do not lean on the level, and do not chase into the gap, the fills get ugly and the stops get run.

THE FULL PICTURE. Depth tells you what is resting, delta tells you who is aggressive, speed tells you how urgent, and the spread tells you how healthy. Read all four and the ladder stops being numbers and starts being a conversation.

## Page 7 (the trigger, no chart)

The DOM is the trigger layer of the whole method. The profiles give you the level, the ladder tells you whether the level is actually being defended.

- Bring a level to the ladder. Never watch the DOM in a vacuum.
- Watch who is aggressive at the level. Are market orders attacking it or drying up? Urgency arriving at your level is the start of a trade.
- Watch what the passive side does. Does resting size hold, reload and stack, or does it pull?
- Score it with delta. Aggression with movement is continuation. Aggression without movement is absorption, somebody big is on the other side.
- Then execute, small and precise. (Size is out of scope. The precision claim is that the DOM is the last gate, not the location-finder.)
