## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists. Mark shelves, ledges, value edges, old highs and lows first. Absorption only means something at a level worth defending. Absorption at a random price means nothing.
2. Reset the DOM **before** price arrives, so the counts are only the fight at the level, not an hour of noise.
3. Ask: who is attacking, and is the level eating it. Big market orders hitting the level that do not push price through it. Aggression arrives, the level eats it, price reverses. Watch the **middle column** (market orders / prints), not the limits. Limits are intentions. Market orders are actions.
4. Wait through the fake break. The DOM shows heavy opposing orders, it looks like the level is about to break. If price rejects fast and moves your way instead, that was institutions absorbing the retail orders. That is the entry. Absorption can spread: 300 buy orders can get eaten across 3 ticks. Watch the zone, not just the single tick.
5. Stacking that **stays** is real defence. Stacking that vanishes as price approaches was never real (pulling). Delta is the referee. Positive delta with no upward progress means trapped buyers, fuel for the other direction. Pass if volume is shrinking (exhaustion) rather than heavy and stuck (absorption).

## Page 3 (no figure)

Lesson 5 gave you the ladder. This lesson gives you the two behaviours on it that actually make entries.

"Absorption is a trap. Retail attacks a level, institutions feed on the attack."

HOW THE TRAP WORKS. Lots of retail traders buy at a key resistance, an old high, expecting the breakout. Big institutions use exactly those buy orders to sell into. The big side has more power, price drops quickly, and the retail buyers are left stuck in losing positions.

THE SIGNATURE. Big market orders hitting your level that do not push price through it. The aggression arrives, the level eats it, price reverses. That is absorption, and it is one of the highest quality triggers on the ladder.

Same trap as trapped-buyers-one-retest, now on the ladder instead of the daily Delta profile.

## Page 5 (no figure)

The passive side of the ladder, two moves.

STACKING. Traders keep adding limit buys on the bid, or limit sells on the ask. Bids stacking while bid delta rises looks like strong buy support. Asks stacking while ask delta rises looks like strong sell pressure.

PULLING. Those stacked orders get removed before price ever trades there. Bids pulling while bid delta is positive means buyers just lost their support, price can drop. Asks pulling while ask delta is negative means sellers lost their pressure, price can rise.

"Stacking shows intent. Pulling shows fluff. Delta confirms who is actually aggressive."

## Page 7 (no figure)

Absorption has a close cousin. They point opposite ways.

ABSORPTION. A passive giant soaks up aggression at a level and holds it. Heavy volume trades, price does not move, the aggressive side gets trapped. It signals a reversal, someone wanted every contract at that price.

EXHAUSTION. The aggressive side simply runs out. Volume dries up, the pushes get smaller, no big passive player is needed, the move just dies of its own accord. It signals a pause or a fade, not necessarily a hard reversal.

Stopping volume is the extreme version: a single burst of huge volume that halts a trend dead. Absorption at its most violent, the last aggressive push meeting a wall, and it often marks the turn.

HOW TO TELL THEM APART. Watch the volume as price stalls. Big volume with no movement is absorption, someone is there. Shrinking volume with no movement is exhaustion, nobody is left. Absorption reverses harder, exhaustion drifts.

Same split as Origin of the Move's aggressive-failure vs passive-failure (tape dies). Absorption is the wall. Exhaustion is the empty tape.
