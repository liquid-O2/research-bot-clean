## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists. Thesis first: auction narrative, volume-profile location, objectives. He tested this feature set without the thesis and it is basically a coin flip. Mechanics second.
2. Ask: is the squeeze **finished**? Aggression that gets nothing is the catalyst, not the entry. Body-print = result. Wick-print = absorbed. The first squeeze fails often. The failure is the setup.
3. Ask: who owns the aggression that built the level. Draw the catalyst at the lowest (or highest) first aggression. The refill clock is the engine: aggressive with no result becomes aggressive with result.
4. Wait for the first squeeze to fail, then wait for the re-squeeze. Early refill below the catalyst is marked as higher-risk, not the default. Median eventual winner in the research dips **18 ticks past the touch** before it works. Acting at the front edge is acting before the trade has started. Rest inside the zone.
5. Only then: ENTRY on the re-squeeze after the failure, protected by the buyers (or sellers) who refilled. SL below (or above) the aggression that built it. If sellers do not come with result, a stop-limit below the wick does not fill. Pass is lawful.

Targets, 1R–3R, trailing convexity, prop-eval math, and account stacking are out of scope. Keep the entry sequence and the 18-tick inside-the-zone fact.

Plain-English block, the terms the figures use:

- **Aggression**: a market order that crosses the spread to get filled now, accepting a worse price for immediacy.
- **Effort vs result**: heavy aggression that moves price has result. Heavy aggression that doesn't is exhaustion.
- **Aggression memory**: price remembers where aggression traded. The research paper's "level memory" is the measured version.
- **Squeeze catalyst**: the cluster of absorbed aggression that sets the squeeze up. You draw it at the lowest (or highest) first aggression.
- **OFM**: Origin of the Move. The squeeze fails, and you enter on the re-squeeze after that failure.
- **Absorption**: aggressive orders soaked up by passive orders. Lots of effort, no movement.
- **Aggression testing**: price returning to an old area of aggression to see if those participants still act. Break through it and they have no result.
- **Squeeze**: repeated aggressive orders getting absorbed, then releasing in one fast move. Watch speed of tape when it goes.
- **Refill clock**: aggressive with no result becomes aggressive with result. The orders that failed reload the next attempt.
- **Speed of tape**: how fast prints are hitting. Spikes when a squeeze releases, dies when one fails passively.

## Pages 15–18 (numbers, not figures)

Research companion *The Refill Effect*: 199 million ticks of NQ and MNQ, 235 sessions, 41,152 zone-touch events.

Entry-side facts, not a score bag:
- Raw order flow alone (delta into the touch, approach speed, imbalance) barely beats a coin flip: **AUC 0.54**. Memory (has this zone been defended before) and location (is it at a real auction edge) carried the signal. Aggression builds the level. The level's history predicts the next touch.
- Fade every touch, no filter: **-0.285R per trade** after costs, 42% of touches hold.
- Grade each touch first, take only the best: **+0.143R per trade** out-of-sample, 79 sessions the model never saw.
- Median eventual winner dips **18 ticks past the touch** before it works. Defence happens **inside** the level, where the refill reloads. Act at the front edge and the data says the trade has not started yet.
- Resting a limit inside the zone: profit factor 1.80. Chasing with market orders, same signals: 0.81 and lost money.

Playbook translations that are entry waits:
- Before acting on a level, ask if it has held before and if it sits at a real auction edge.
- Wait for the second test, or for memory, construction, and location to agree. The first touch of a fresh zone is the weakest version.
- Rest the order inside the zone, not at its edge. Research config rests 12 ticks in with a 32-tick stop; the depth matters less than the principle.
- Be the order that gets filled by the flush, not the order that pays the spread to chase it.

p18, read twice: **there is no mechanical entry signal in here.** When the entry was rebuilt from scratch and tested causally, with every trace of hindsight stripped out, it came back negative, on the order of -0.16R to -0.54R out-of-sample. What survives is a grading system for touches you have already found, and an execution rule about which side of the book to stand on. The thesis is not optional. Prop-eval pass rates, -4R daily stops, payout caps, and three staggered accounts are out of scope.

This PDF's decision is ordered: thesis location → absorbed cluster (catalyst) → first squeeze fails → refill clock → ENTRY on the re-squeeze (or the passive tape-dies variant), order resting **inside** the zone, stop beyond the aggression that built it. Gap **G1** is still the price-row of that aggression. Gap **G3**: failed-squeeze then re-squeeze as a wait, not a score. The 18-tick inside-the-touch dip is a location of the fill, not a new indicator.
