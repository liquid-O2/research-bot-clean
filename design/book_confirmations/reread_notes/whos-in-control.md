## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists: a higher-timeframe balance with real extremes, redrawn until it actually fits price.
2. Ask: is this auction **finished** at the extreme? AMT gives the shape, not the outcome. The extreme by itself decides nothing.
3. Ask: who owns **control**, not the last wick. How price arrived (aggressive push = that side wants to defend; slow grind = expect it broken). On Delta, an aggressive push that **fades** is trapped positioning, not strength. Highest Delta on the profile (G1) is the same question this PDF asks in HTF language.
4. Wait. Chop at an old balance with neither side holding a push is a pass, not a fade. A break of a previous balance is not the trade. The retest that holds is.
5. Only then: drop to LTF (his default 15 minute) and take the trigger, or pass. HTF is a bias until LTF agrees. If the retest fails to hold, flip the read rather than force the original one.

Waiting through a range that gave nothing is lawful. The strongest trade in the session was the second retest after sellers had already proven control once, not the first place that looked tradeable.

## Page 3 (no figure)

AMT gives the shape of the range, not the outcome. Price trending up into an extreme "should" come back down. If sellers are not actually present, the more likely outcome is break and continuation, with or without a retest, sometimes with no retest at all.

Same logic both ways. At a high, if sellers are stronger than the buyers pushing in, price most likely comes back down. If buyers are the stronger side, price is more likely to break out, retest, and continue higher.

> "There really is no way of knowing. Some of the ways I like to understand it is the aggression." (p3)

## Page 4 (no figure)

First filter, before any level or retest. Same extreme, two arrivals, two situations.

Aggressive: buyers pushing in hard and fast. That side wants higher prices and is likely to defend the balance once it gets there. Reached aggressively, expect it defended.

Slow grind: no real interest in holding prices up there. Hands the opposition an opening to step in, take control, and push back through the whole range. Reached slowly, expect it broken.

## Page 5 (no figure)

Second read, for when you missed the decision. You do not know whether sellers were absorbed at a high or genuinely took control. Use a previous balance.

If price trends, fails to break the previous balance's low, and comes back up, buyers are most likely in control. No short is valid yet.

A short only becomes valid once price **breaks that low, comes back up for a genuine retest, and then continues lower**. That sequence confirms sellers, not the break by itself.

If sellers are not strong enough to push through on that retest, the read flips: buyers absorb them, or sellers have no real interest in pushing lower, and buyers drive price back up.

## Page 10 (no figure)

The strongest trade was not the first place that looked tradeable. It was the retest after sellers had already proven they could take control once. Everywhere else in the range, control was "free game." Down at the level that had already been broken and retested, sellers had already demonstrated the read, and the second retest repeated it.

> even when the higher timeframe gives you a reaction, you need to confirm it, back it up by checking whether the lower timeframe is actually showing you that price wants to go the direction the higher timeframe suggested. A bias without that confirmation is a guess wearing a framework. It's all about alignment. (p10, paraphrase of his close; the "free game" phrase is his)

## Page 11 checklist (entry-side)

READING THE EXTREME
- I've checked whether price arrived aggressively (defend it) or slowly (expect it broken).
- I'm not assuming rejection or acceptance from the extreme alone.

USING A PREVIOUS BALANCE
- I have a breakout AND a retest that held, not just the breakout.
- If the retest failed to hold, I've flipped my read to the other side rather than forcing the original one.

TRAPPED POSITIONING
- I can point to an aggressive push on Delta that visibly faded, not just assume exhaustion.
- I understand that trapped positioning fuels the move against it, not just fails to stop it.

WHEN THE HIGHER TIMEFRAME GIVES NOTHING
- I'm willing to take no trade through a genuine chop, rather than force one.
- I've dropped to a lower timeframe (his default: 15 minute) for confirmation before acting on a higher timeframe bias.

## What we implemented wrong

Without G1 (delta-by-price histogram) you cannot see the HTF fade as a **price row**. Without a retest-after-break as a hard wait, the break itself gets taken.
