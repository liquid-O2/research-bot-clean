## Sequence (not a score bag)

1. Location already exists: a real extreme (shelf, ledge, low volume node, minor volume node), not POC, not the middle of balance, not today's still-moving VAH/VAL.
2. Ask: is this auction **finished**? Current-day VAH/VAL recalculate as the day develops. Previous day's VAH/VAL are closed and stay put. Absorption logic assumes the level is fixed.
3. Ask: who owns the **highest Delta print** where control changed hands, not the last wick. Effort into a passive wall with no reward. If CVD is still above its median, directional Delta is still bullish whatever one absorption print says.
4. Wait for a **reward system**: price must move in your direction within 3 ticks of the absorption print. Enter on the retest of that reward system, not on the wall itself. A second, opposite aggression has to show up, not just an absence of the first side.
5. Only then: entry / wait / pass. Filter any print sitting at POC regardless of how clean it looks. Absorption is a reversal tool. You do not use it to catch a continuation.

The four mistakes are four checks on one question: has this absorption been confirmed, or did you just see a print.

## Page 8 (no figure)

Absorption as a sponge, taken literally.

Price pushes up aggressively, a ton of effort from buyers. Instantly hits a wall. That wall is **passive sellers**, sitting there soaking up the aggression. You will often see a bubble of aggressive buying print right at that wall, then price goes lower anyway.

The test: are the buyers being rewarded for that effort. If price goes up on effort and gets nothing, that is the tell. Then a second signal has to show up: seller aggression, actual reward on the other side, before the reversal is more than a hope.

## Page 13 checklist (entry-side)

MISTAKE 1: WAITING FOR A REWARD SYSTEM
- Price has moved in my direction within 3 ticks of the absorption print.
- Time and sales or speed of tape shows the opposing side is not being refreshed, not defended.
- Price is on the correct side of the CVD median for the direction I'm about to take.
- I'm using this to fade an existing move, not to catch a continuation of one.

MISTAKE 2: FOLLOWING THE AUCTION
- This absorption is at a real extreme (shelf, ledge, low volume node, minor volume node), not near POC or inside balance.
- If I'm using VAH/VAL, I know whether it's today's (still moving) or a previous day's (fixed).

MISTAKE 3: THE DEFINITION ITSELF
- I can point to the aggressive effort, then the passive wall that absorbed it, in that order.
- That effort was NOT rewarded, confirmed by price failing to hold the push.
- A second, opposite aggression has shown up, not just an absence of the first side.
- I'm entering on the retest of the reward system, not on the wall itself.

MISTAKE 4: YOUR SIDE WINNING, NOT JUST THE OTHER SIDE LOSING
- I've filtered out any print sitting at POC, regardless of how clean it looks.
- I can trace the wall back to an actual Delta spike where control changed hands.
- If this is a failed auction, I'm treating the return-to-balance odds as roughly 72 to 80 percent, not a fixed number.

## What we implemented wrong

We scored "some absorption" as an independent feature. The PDF's decision is ordered and location-specific: finished extreme → effort then passive wall with no reward → 3-tick replenish → second aggression → enter on the retest of the reward system. Gap **G1** (delta-by-price) is how you find the spike that marks control changing. Gap **G2**: the 3-tick reward window after the print. We never waited. We treated the print as the entry.
