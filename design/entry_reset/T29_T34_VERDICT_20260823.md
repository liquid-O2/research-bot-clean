# Tickets 29 and 34 — the wait cannot be priced, and arming does not replace it

## Ticket 29: what the age-180 proxy actually hides

Established from source, not assumed:

- **The label window is entry-anchored on the left.** `current_entry_usd` is the
  signed P&L from the row's own snapshot to a mostly fixed right edge
  (`exit_ts_recv_ns`, the phase close, or a censor)
  (`exact_delayed_teacher.py:1029`, `causal_label_atlas.py:846`). A later entry
  therefore shortens the window against the same exit; it does not shift it. So
  only a fresh label is honest, and a drift correction is not valid.
- **The labelled age grid stops at 600 s.** `confirmation.training_offsets_seconds`
  refuses any expiry but 300 or 600, and this matrix carries 0-300 s. The hold
  enters at 7,380 s (HG, NKD) and 10,980 s (SI).

Measured on the hold's own picks:

- **The trade is still open at real entry.** Occupancy at the 180 s row is a
  median 17,000-25,000 s, so 94-100% of picks still have 9,700-18,100 s of
  window left when the rule would enter. The proxy is not cashing closed
  positions.
- **So the error is pure entry-price drift, and it is unmeasurable here.** Over
  the ages the matrix does carry, y is nearly flat in entry age (HG mean $15.1
  at age 0 versus $15.8 at 240 s), but its p90 decays about $0.10 per second.
  Linear extrapolation of that to a 7,200 s wait is roughly $750 against HG's
  $1,610. That extrapolation is a BOUND, not a correction, and it is large
  enough to decide the rule.

**Verdict: the ticket-28 hold cannot be priced on this data.** Pricing it needs
labels at the real entry ages, which means running the universe at new snapshot
offsets. That is a corpus build, not a probe.

## Ticket 34: do not wait to enter, arm and take the next name

If the wait is the unpriceable part, remove the wait from the ENTRY while
keeping it in the DECISION. Rule: hold the running phase extreme as before, but
when it has been unbeaten for H, arm the phase and enter the first name that
becomes eligible afterwards, at its own 180 s of age. Every entry is then
exactly labelled.

**It fails, and not narrowly.** TRAIN best and both held blocks:

| Asset | TRAIN best (H) | THRESHOLD | FORWARD | Null p97.5 at that H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HG | $575 (20 min) | -$340 | -$85 | $604 |
| NKD | ~$26 | ~-$184 | -$235 | at or above cash |
| SI | $712 (5 min) | $265 | $103 | $731 |

Every arm sits at or inside its null. Letter `armed_insufficient` on all three
assets and both held blocks.

**What that tells us, and it is the useful part.** The hold's value is not a
timing signal. Knowing that the phase extreme has settled buys nothing on its
own: the next name to form is unrelated to it. The value lives entirely in the
IDENTITY of the held name. So the 2-hour wait is doing the work, and the wait is
exactly the part that cannot be priced.

## The opening this leaves

Tickets 25 and 26 concluded that the prefix cannot identify the paying name, and
that scan ranked matrix columns by their RAW value. The corrected score is not a
raw column: it is the side-resolved extreme, `long_min_short_min`, which ranks
longs and shorts in opposite directions on the same column. A raw-column scan
cannot express that, so **ticket 26's negative does not cover the corrected
score.** Under it the Stage A oracle went from missing every rung to clearing
HG's by 2.1-3.2 SE.

That makes the next test obvious and cheap: run the identification in the prefix
frame with the corrected side-resolved score, entering at each name's own 180 s
of age, where the label is exact. Ticket 35.
