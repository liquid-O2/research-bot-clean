## CORRECTED the same day, and the correction matters

The first draft of this document said the pool is "one winner and five losers"
and that the goal needs picking the exact best. **That overstated the
difficulty.** Reading `by_rank_mean_usd` out of the same receipt shows the payoff
decays SMOOTHLY, and ranks 0-2 are non-negative on every asset:

| Asset | r0 | r1 | r2 | r3 | r4 | r5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HG TRAIN | $924 | $431 | -$2 | -$240 | -$490 | -$671 |
| NKD TRAIN | $617 | $378 | $127 | -$50 | -$303 | -$509 |
| SI TRAIN | $799 | $447 | $235 | -$49 | -$376 | -$648 |

Held blocks hold the same shape. So it is roughly half winners and half losers,
not five to one, and the corrected target is much better posed:

| Asset | Need per trade | Top-2 mean | Top-3 mean | All-6 mean | Live arm (TRAIN) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HG | $667 | **$678** | $451 | -$95 | $333 |
| NKD | $500 | $498 | $374 | -$51 | $292 |
| SI | $500 | **$623** | $494 | -$71 | $488 |

**The target, stated properly: land in the TOP TWO of about six events,
reliably.** That clears HG and SI outright and comes within $2 on NKD. Landing
uniformly in the top THREE is not enough on any asset. Picking at random is
catastrophic.

And the live arm is behind even that. HG's picker banks $333 per trade against a
top-3 mean of $451 — **it is worse than picking uniformly among the top three**.
SI's $488 is between top-3 and top-2, and is 2% short of its rung on TRAIN
before falling to $374 and $289 on the held blocks.

So the failure has two distinguishable parts, and only now can they be told
apart:

1. **The picker is weak.** On HG it does not even reach uniform-top-3.
2. **What edge it has does not survive out of sample.** SI goes $488 TRAIN to
   $374 THRESHOLD to $289 FORWARD against a flat $500 requirement.

Part 2 is a resolution and generalisation problem, which is what the 2,788-session
corpus is for. Part 1 is the live question.

**Capacity is NOT the constraint.** The top-2 oracle banks $4,064 (HG), $2,984
(NKD), $3,738 (SI) per asset-day at 6 trades — roughly twice every rung. Even
inside the 12-trade portfolio cap (two entries in one cell per asset, one in the
others) HG's oracle is $3,203 a day against a $2,000 rung. There is far more
money in the candidate set than the goal needs.

## The one-line answer

**The candidate pool has a negative mean because its bottom half is deeply
negative, so the goal requires landing in the TOP TWO of about six events
reliably — and the current picker does not reach even uniform-top-three.**

That is why every selector has come out null. It is not that the signal is
absent. It is that the accuracy required is extreme, and the reason it is
extreme has never been stated: **the non-winners are negative.**

## Your question about the hold, answered

You asked whether the hold captures anything if it identifies the candidate
forty minutes late. Measured, and the answer is no.

| Asset | Hold's pick, mean y | Cell best, mean y | Hold gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| HG | $511-546 | $876-959 | 55-58% |
| NKD | $133-254 | $572-620 | 23-41% |
| SI | $310-443 | $694-907 | 37-51% |

**The hold is not picking the payer.** It picks a name worth about half the best,
and on NKD barely a quarter.

And the structure makes it worse, exactly as you suspected. The hold fires only
when nothing has beaten the running extreme for H seconds — so by construction
the price has NOT gone further in the extending direction, and the best price
still on the table at fire time is worse than the extreme's own. Measured, that
retracement is a median of **$37-74** and a p90 of **$88-233**, on picks whose
whole edge is a few hundred dollars.

So the hold pays twice for its patience: it selects a mediocre name, and then it
enters that name at a worse price than the name itself ever offered. The T28
number was never the payer's value. It was the value of a name that had merely
survived.

**Ticket 49 (price the hold on exact labels) is therefore demoted.** Exact labels
would sharpen a number whose selection is already known to be poor. It stays as
a control, not as the needle.

## What this changes about where to look

Every attempt so far has been a RANKER: find the best of six. Against a pool
with a negative mean, that is the hardest possible framing, because it demands
being right about the top rather than merely right about the bottom.

**The unexplored direction is elimination.** Removing events that lose is a
different problem from finding the one that wins:

- It has a 51-59% base rate, not a 17% one.
- It tolerates error very differently. Dropping a winner costs its y; keeping a
  loser costs its y. Both are a few hundred dollars, but there are five losers
  for every winner, so precision on the loser side has five times the leverage.
- Every measurement this program has run tested the winner side. Ticket 23's
  `good_enough` screen asked "is there a name worth $600" — about CELLS. Tickets
  25, 26, 36 asked "which name is the best" — the ranker framing. **Nothing has
  ever asked "which of these will lose".**

That is a genuinely new, well-posed question over data already on disk, and it
does not need the corpus, the late ages, or R6.

## What is NOT the problem, restated with its receipt

- Not the generator. The cell best clears the per-trade requirement on all three
  assets.
- Not the model family. Trees lost to unit-weight Dawes on this plane.
- Not the label at <= 300 s. It is exact and the payoff table above is built on it.
- Not identification of the FRAME. Recall of the payer inside the event set is
  1.000.
- Not lookahead. Audited in ticket 44.

## The next measurement, and it is cheap

Ticket 51: the loser screen. Over the event set, ask whether any column or small
composite separates y < 0 from y > 0 at each event's own DELTA_SEC row, under
the ticket-44 collinearity control against entry price. Report it as precision
and recall on the LOSER class, and cash the rule that drops the predicted losers

If a loser screen lifts the pool mean above zero, the ranking problem becomes
easy and the rung comes into range. If nothing separates losers either, then the
plane is closed for both framings, and that is a conclusive answer rather than
another null.
