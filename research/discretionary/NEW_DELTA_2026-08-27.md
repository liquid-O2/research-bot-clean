# Terminality-confirmation playbook and the three-PDF delta

Status date: 2026-08-27. A distillation of source material into computable form.
It is a hypothesis catalog, not a measured result, and not authorization to trade
anything.

Baseline is `design/ENTRY_V2_DISCRETIONARY_FEATURE_CROSSWALK.md`, which already
distilled 31 PDFs into feature families and the decision grammar. Where a concept
is already implemented there, this document cites the feature prefix rather than
re-deriving it. The three PDFs new since that audit
(`a-clean-continuation-short`, `amt-on-live-markets`,
`reading-the-volume-profile`) were read complete with diagrams; the priority set
was re-read at the diagram level only to make the playbook concrete.

The problem this serves: the quiet+bounce detector finds terminal extremes (hit
0.75-0.82) but fades the wrong side roughly half the time (side_hit 0.36-0.57).
What follows is the arbiter and the confirmation-quality layer, as minute-scale
arithmetic.

**Correction to the dispatching brief:** `fp-lesson-8` is footprint basics
(diagonal imbalance, stacked imbalances). `fp-lesson-9` is absorption and the POC
flip. The mapping was inverted; all absorption and POC-flip material below comes
from lesson 9.

## The library's own answer to the side problem

Two independent statements, one qualitative and one measured, say the same thing.

`a-clean-continuation-short` takes three entries in one session and states the
filter plainly: the higher-timeframe thesis was short from the open, so every
entry had to agree with that bias before it was considered at all, and a clean
squeeze in the opposite direction would have been ignored — not because the read
was wrong on its own terms, but because it disagreed with context already
established. The extreme supplies timing and confirmation quality. It does not
supply side.

`refill-effect` puts a number on it. Over 41,152 touches across 235 sessions of
NQ and MNQ, fading every touch indiscriminately returns **-0.285R per trade after
costs**, and only 42% of touches hold. Selecting touches with a graded model
returns **+0.143R out-of-sample, PF 1.19, AUC 0.63** against a scrambled-label
placebo at 0.51. The decisive detail is the family ablation: **flow-family
features alone score AUC 0.54**, barely better than guessing, while memory and
location do almost all the work. The paper's own summary is that aggression
builds the level but it is the level's memory that predicts the next touch.

A side-blind fade at a detected extreme is therefore expected to run near
coin-flip, which is what side_hit 0.36-0.57 is. The arbiter needs a **slow term**
computed away from the extreme. Local flow sharpens an existing bias; it cannot
manufacture one. This is the single most important claim in this document, and it
is the one with independent measured support.

## Notation

All series are per-minute and causal. For series `X` at minute `t`:

- `pct(X,t)` is the expanding-window percentile of `X_t` among `X_1..X_t`, the
  session so far only.
- `med(X,t)` is the expanding-window median.

Every threshold below is a percentile or a ratio. No absolute contract-specific
number appears anywhere; the few absolute figures quoted from sources are marked
as source values and are starting points for fitting, never constants.

The extreme is detected at minute `t0` on side `s` in {`low`,`high`}. Fading a low
is a long; fading a high is a short. Windows:

- `A` (approach) is the `K` minutes ending at `t0`, `K` about 5.
- `Q` (quiet) is the detector's own quiet interval after `t0`.
- `C` (confirm) runs from the end of `Q` out to the sweep-4 delay budget: roughly
  20 minutes on NKD, 45 on SI. Waiting inside `C` is nearly free, which is what
  makes a `wait` tier affordable rather than a missed trade.

Series in hand: `delta`, `vol`, `ntrades`, `maxtrade`, `quote_events`,
`attack_low/high`, `yield_low/high`, `reload_low/high`, `twoside`. Three derived
quantities carry most of the new weight:

- `conc = maxtrade / vol`, concentration of a minute's volume in its largest print.
- `churn = quote_events / ntrades`, quote updates per trade.
- `pace = vol / (minutes spent traversing the last leg into the extreme)`, the
  arrival-speed term for pattern 6.

## Pattern 1: absorption at a level

**Discretionary definition.** Aggressive effort from one side pushes into a
level, meets a passive wall, is *not rewarded* — price fails to hold the push —
and then opposite aggression arrives. `fp-lesson-9` gives the sharpest test:
**the candle and the delta disagree.** Bearish absorption is a down candle on
positive delta; bullish absorption is an up candle on negative delta. Agreement is
explicitly not a signal: "the disagreement IS the signal, agreement is just a
trend." `your-mistakes-with-absorption` scopes it hard — absorption is a
**reversal** tool only, never a continuation tool, which is exactly our use.

**Computable definition.** `yield_s` is already adverse ticks per unit attack
volume, so the effort-without-result core is a direct conjunction:

```
absorb_s = pct(attack_s, t0) >= 0.75  AND  pct(yield_s, t0) <= 0.25
```

Reinforce with replenishment, `pct(reload_s / attack_s, t0) >= 0.75`, and with the
sign disagreement where a per-minute open and close are available:

```
disagree = sign(close - open) opposite to sign(delta), same minute
```

The disagreement test is cheap and is the source's primary discriminator, so a
per-minute open/close is worth caching for it alone if not already present.

**Two hard location gates, both from the sources, both cheap.**

- **Absorption at or near POC is not absorption.** The mistakes document filters
  it out entirely "regardless of how clean the print looks," because POC and
  balance is the area price is statistically most likely to chop inside rather
  than reject from. The signature must sit at a real extreme — shelf, ledge, low
  volume node, minor volume node.
- **Current-day VAH and VAL are not levels.** They are lagging values that
  recalculate as the session develops. Only the prior session's closed, fixed
  value-area edges are usable as level references. Any feature that anchors to a
  developing same-day VA edge is anchoring to a moving target.

**Side verdict.** Absorption at the low means sellers are being absorbed by
passive buyers, so buyers hold the level and the fade is long. At the high, the
mirror.

**Already implemented:** `disc_absorption_`, `disc_state_price_yield_`.

## Pattern 2: the reward system, and why entering at the print loses

**Discretionary definition.** The absorption print alone is not the trade. The
confirmation the source calls the *reward system* is a three-tick replenishment in
your direction within three ticks of the absorption printing. Without it,
**roughly 27% of absorptions fail**, and entering on the print itself is named as
the single most common mistake, ahead of the other three combined. Entry is on the
retest of the reward system, not on the wall.

`refill-effect` measures the same thing from the execution side. The median
eventual winner **first dips 18 ticks past the touch before it works** — adverse
excursion is expected, not disqualifying. Its entry is a resting limit 12 ticks
*inside* the level, and the comparison against chasing is stark: resting inside
gives PF 1.80 and 68.8% wins on 64 fills, while a market order at the touch gives
PF 0.81 and 27.2% wins on 312 fills. The limit fills less often precisely because
it demands the flush that defines the setup.

**Computable definition.** Do not treat `t0` as the entry minute. Require, inside
`C`:

```
reward = price displacement favourable to the fade, normalized by the
         session's typical minute range, exceeding a small positive
         threshold, sustained for >= 2 consecutive minutes
retest = one subsequent approach to the level that does not exceed the
         extreme, with attack_retest / attack_flush <= 0.6
```

**Implication for the mill.** The detector currently fades at the detected
extreme. Both sources say that is the losing entry, and one of them measures the
gap at PF 1.80 versus 0.81 on otherwise identical signals. Modelling entry as
*resting inside the level and tolerating an adverse excursion* rather than *acting
at the extreme* is a change to the entry law, not a feature, and it is testable
independently of everything else here.

**Already implemented:** `disc_state_`, `disc_path_` carry the ordered
reclaim/lift/retest components. The reward-system window is a new composite.

## Pattern 3: trapped-trader flush and the one retest

**Discretionary definition.** Buyers who buy into a high and then watch price turn
become **forced sellers** — exiting is not optional, it is how a losing trade
closes. A heavy one-sided delta print at an extreme is therefore a flag for
trapped positioning, not a reason to join that side. `trapped-buyers-one-retest`
requires the level to have **already failed twice, in two different sessions**,
before it is tradeable at all: one failed push is noise, two is the market saying
where it will not trade. Then a breakout, then **exactly one** retest. No retest,
no trade — the stated plan if the retest does not come is to stand aside.

**Computable definition.** A flush minute `f` is
`pct(vol,f) >= 0.9 AND pct(|delta|,f) >= 0.9` with `sign(delta_f)` pressing into
the extreme. Trapped inventory proxy `T` is the absolute sum of `delta` over the
minutes spent beyond the level. Reclaim requires `delta` to flip sign and
accumulate to at least roughly `0.5 * T` back inside the level. The retest then
applies the pattern-2 test.

**Retest budgets disagree across sources, deliberately.** Record all three rather
than averaging them: absorption allows **one** more retest after the reward system
confirms; trapped-buyers requires **two prior-session failures plus exactly one**
retest; `refill-effect` sets **no cap** on touch count — prior defence is a
positive scored feature — but bounds each event with a 30-minute cancel and a
32-tick stop. The reconcilable core is that repetition at a level is evidence
*for* the level, while repetition of a *failing entry* at that level is evidence
against the read.

**Side verdict.** The trapped side is the one whose `delta` pressed into the
extreme during the flush. The market leaves against them. This is the cleanest
side read available because it is a statement about inventory that must be
unwound.

**Already implemented:** `disc_state_`, `disc_path_` (failed reclaim, trapped
inventory continuation).

## Pattern 4: refill/iceberg defense and the repetition rule

**Discretionary definition.** The defence happens *inside* the zone, not at its
front edge. `refill-effect`'s order-book schematic is three panels: resting size
stacked across five price rows; then aggressive sells consume the front two rows
as price dips into the zone; then those same two rows return at full size as fresh
orders replace the filled ones, and price bounces. The level remembers who
defended it because those participants are still there.

`a-clean-continuation-short` supplies the sharper qualifying rule: a refill zone is
a level where **the same side keeps winning the argument**, and the evidence is
repetition, not a single instance. It is equally explicit that a stop-out at the
level does not disqualify re-entry so long as the level's behaviour has not
actually changed.

**Computable definition.** Replenishment intensity is
`pct(reload_s / attack_s, t0) >= 0.75`, reinforced by `pct(churn, t0) >= 0.70` —
many quote updates per trade is the MBP-1 shadow of size being restored after each
fill.

The **level-owner counter** is the part worth building fresh. Bin the session into
price bands of roughly the detector's level tolerance. For every prior visit to a
band, record which side won it: the sign of the band's accumulated `delta` against
the direction price ultimately left. Require the same side to have won at least
twice before treating the band as a refill zone. The counter's sign is the band's
owner, and its magnitude is the slow term's confidence.

**Grade monotonicity is measured**, which matters for tiering: hold rate by model
grade runs from 25% in the worst decile to 63% in the best, monotonically. A
graded arbiter is worth building even if its middle deciles are useless.

**Side verdict.** The band's owner is the side that has repeatedly won there.
Computed away from the current extreme, which is what makes it the slow term.

**Already implemented:** `disc_level_`, `disc_absorption_`, `disc_test_`,
`disc_quote_`. The repeated-visit **win counter with a same-side minimum of two**
is not in the crosswalk and should be added.

## Pattern 5: delta divergence, and the two dominance rules

**Discretionary definition.** Price makes a new extreme and the flow does not
confirm it — the move "is running on fumes." The **exhaustion print** is the
extreme form: a huge delta bar prints and price refuses to extend, meaning the
last aggressive push was absorbed, and **the turn is usually one or two candles
away.** That is the only explicit timing constraint in the whole library, and at
one-minute scale it is directly usable.

Two dominance rules decide which divergences resolve and which get run over:

- **Highest-delta-print dominance** (`reading-delta`). Compare the current print
  against the *maximum* of the session's delta profile. If the profile's peak sits
  on the side you were about to fade, the read flips and you default to the peak.
  The aggression you saw was real; it just was not the side that got rewarded.
- **CVD median.** If price sits on the wrong side of the cumulative-delta median
  for your direction, the trend is intact and the counter-trade fails. A CVD leg
  that merely *tests* the median without breaking it is not a transfer of control.

**The new size-versus-count split.** The platform readout in
`a-clean-continuation-short` carries four separate totals — cumulative volume,
volume delta, cumulative trade count, and trade-count delta — and on that session
the two deltas **disagree in sign**: volume delta negative while count delta is
positive. Many small buys against fewer, larger sells. The same idea drives the
size-scaled aggressor bubbles that define the origin level: the level is drawn
where the *large* prints landed, not where the most prints landed. Size and count
are different witnesses, and when they disagree the size witness is right about
who will still be there in an hour.

```
crowd      : pct(ntrades,A) >= 0.80  AND  pct(conc,A) <= 0.35
initiative : pct(conc,A)    >= 0.75
```

A crowd-signature push is the population that gets trapped, and fading it is the
trade. Live initiative still producing yield means the move is unfinished and the
extreme is not terminal. Initiative that has *stopped* producing yield is
absorption, which is the strongest confirmation available.

**Already implemented:** `disc_footprint_`, `disc_level_` carry max event size.
The count-versus-size **contrast** and the highest-delta dominance rule are new.

## Pattern 6: who is in control — the arbiter

**Arrival mode is the first filter.** `whos-in-control` applies it before any
level or retest read: aggressive, fast arrival into an extreme means that side
wants those prices and will defend them; slow, grinding arrival means no real
interest. Same level, two arrival modes, two opposite expectations. The document
is qualitative throughout — it contains no numeric threshold anywhere — and the
*direction* of the mapping is stated ambiguously enough that it should be fit
rather than assumed. What is unambiguous and valuable is that **arrival mode is a
first-order discriminator of whether an extreme is terminal**, and it is cheaply
computable from `pace`, `conc` and `churn` over `A`.

**The control rule proper.** Sellers control when the low breaks, price returns for
a genuine retest, and *then continues lower from the retest*; the retest holding
is the confirmation, not the break. Buyers control when price fails to break the
prior balance's low and comes back up, or when sellers break it, retest, and
cannot push through — at which point the read **flips**. The stand-aside case is
named explicitly: when neither side can push and hold, and the pattern simply
repeats, "that's not indecision to trade around, it's the market explicitly not
giving you anything yet." No trades were taken through that entire stretch.

**Finished versus unfinished auction — the `twoside` reading.** `fp-lesson-9`'s
footprint screenshots mark, per minute, whether the bar's extreme tick traded on
both sides (unfinished auction, a magnet to be repaired) or had a hard zero on one
side (finished auction, a one-sided sweep). The decoded sequence is the single
most concrete terminality evidence in the library, and it runs against a naive
reading of `twoside`:

In the 14:39-14:47 sell-off, every intermediate **high** was unfinished with
buyers lifting the offer there (+30, +34, +34, +33) while the bars themselves
netted -496 and -550 — textbook bearish absorption, and none of those were the
turn. The turn came at the **14:43 bar, delta -674**, which contained three
enormous two-sided rows near its low with almost no net (`436x424`, `399x412`,
`357x330`), then a final one-sided push (`307x114`), and terminated in a
**finished** low (`21x0`, unmarked). The very next minute printed **+332**,
including a 7.4x buy-side row. Sellers tried once more for only -48, that attempt
left an unfinished low, and the following minute came back +244.

So the terminal extreme was a *finished* one-sided print at the end of a
maximum-delta attack bar, followed by a delta sign flip within one minute — while
the *non*-terminal extremes were the two-sided ones. `twoside` at the extreme
minute is therefore a signal whose sign must be tested, not assumed, and the
composite worth testing is: max-percentile `|delta|`, bottom-percentile yield,
`twoside` false at the extreme tick, delta flip within one to two minutes.

**Confidence tiering is stated verbatim** in `fp-lesson-9` and maps onto our
tiers: "**One tell is interest, two is a trade.** Absorption alone says someone is
defending. Absorption plus the flip plus divergence says they are winning."

**Computable combination.** Three signed terms, conjunction rather than vote, unit
weights per house doctrine:

```
slow  = sign of the level-owner counter at the extreme's band, backed by
        profile shape. Computed away from t0.
local = +1 toward the fade if absorb_s holds, or a trapped-flush reclaim
        completed, or the exhaustion-print composite fired
conf  = +1 if the reward system confirmed and one retest held with decaying
        attack, and POC migration points away from the extreme
```

**Already implemented:** `disc_behavior_`, `disc_tape_`, `disc_auction_`.

## Pattern 7: POC flip and the 80/20 traverse rule

**Discretionary definition.** `fp-lesson-9` defines the POC flip precisely and at
the right scale: every candle has its own point of control, the price inside it
where the most volume traded, and a **POC flip is that busiest price jumping to
the other side of the candle** — control changed hands. Migration is read *between
consecutive candles*, not within one. Its diagram shows two adjacent volume
histograms: in the first the brightest bar is second from the bottom, labelled
sellers in control; in the second the brightest bar is second from the top,
labelled buyers take over, with an arrow running diagonally between them. At a key
level, a flip in your direction confirms the level is being defended. The stack
order is fixed: **level, then absorption, then flip** — the flip is the trailing
confirmation, never the trigger. No invalidation rule is given beyond the stack
failing to complete.

`amt-on-live-markets` supplies the session-scale companion, as odds: once price has
failed an auction at one extreme and returned into the prior range, roughly 80% of
the time it traverses to the far extreme and 20% it ranges inside. The tell for
which case is POC behaviour — repeated failure to break and hold through the POC
is the ranging case, an aggressive push through it with a holding retest is the
traverse case. The checklist names the failure mode: check POC behaviour rather
than defaulting to the 80% case.

**Computable definition.**

```
minute POC flip : argmax-volume price row within minute t moves from the
                  lower half to the upper half of the bar (or the reverse)
                  between consecutive minutes, in the fade's direction
traverse gate   : after the fade begins, count POC crossings that hold
                  (price beyond POC for >= 2 consecutive minutes) versus
                  rejections (touch and return within 1 minute).
                  >= 1 hold with decaying opposite attack -> far-extreme target
                  >= 2 rejections                          -> range case, near target
```

**Dependency, stated rather than assumed.** Both readings need per-minute volume
binned by price, which the listed series do not provide. The minute-scale flip
needs intra-minute price rows; the session-scale POC needs a developing profile.
If neither is cached, the honest degradation is a volume-weighted price centroid
and its migration sign — weaker, and it should be labelled as a proxy. Do not
fabricate a profile from series that cannot support one.

**Side and target verdict.** Migration away from the extreme confirms side;
traverse behaviour sets the objective. The same fade is a scratch in the range
case and a full trip in the traverse case, so this pattern is where the dollars
are decided even when the side is already right.

**Already implemented:** `disc_auction_` covers POC/VA/VWAP migration and value
acceptance. The minute-scale flip and the 80/20 break-and-hold counter are new.

## Pattern 8: profile shape as a regime gate, with a hard abstain

`reading-the-volume-profile` supplies a five-shape taxonomy the crosswalk's
balance-fade-versus-expansion split does not fully carry, and one shape is an
instruction to not trade.

- **Balanced** — symmetric bell, volume concentrated centrally, tapering evenly at
  both ends, price playing end to end with neither side sustaining control. The
  author calls these the strongest and most identifiable levels in the market.
  *This is the regime the fade belongs in.*
- **Double distribution** — two shelves joined by a thin leg. The diagram marks
  the entry as a circled point at the retest of a shelf boundary with the target
  arrow pointing at the far shelf, both marked in advance. *Best target geometry
  available.*
- **Trending** — a long narrow spread with no bell anywhere, price stair-stepping
  continuously with only small counter-trend wiggles. The instruction is to get
  off; there is nothing to trade. The named account-destroying mistake is exactly
  ours: repeatedly fading small reactions inside a trend until the account is
  gone. *Hard abstain.*
- **P-shape** — thin stem below, wide bulge at the top; underlying trend reads
  higher. **B-shape** — the mirror; underlying trend reads lower. Both supply a
  cheap **directional prior** for the slow term.

**Computable classification** from the volume-by-price distribution: concentration
of volume around the modal band, position of the modal band within the session
range, and count of distinct modal clusters. Trending is detectable without a full
profile — range expanding near-monotonically, `twoside` rarely set, and no band
accumulating repeated two-sided visits. A market that has established no fair
value has no extreme to leave.

**Regime dependence is measured and adverse.** `refill-effect` discloses that its
most recent quarter, a compressed-volatility regime, **lost money at every
parameter setting tried** — PF 0.76. Whatever is built here needs a regime gate
that can turn it off, and the profile-shape classifier is the cheapest candidate.

## Confidence tiers

**act** — slow, local and conf all agree, which is the sources' "two tells or
better." Concretely: level-owner counter at two or more same-side wins pointing
the way the fade points; shape balanced or double-distribution; absorption or a
completed trapped-flush reclaim or the exhaustion-print composite; the reward
system confirmed and one retest held with decaying attack; POC migration away from
the extreme. Objective is the far shelf in the double-distribution case, otherwise
whatever the traverse counter implies.

**wait** — the extreme and the absorption are present but the reward system has
not confirmed, or the slow term is neutral, or initiative is still producing
yield. One tell is interest, not a trade. Waiting costs almost nothing inside the
measured delay budget, and `amt-on-live-markets` makes the same point
qualitatively with a live example of a real rejection that simply took longer to
confirm than the textbook version.

**stand aside** — any one of: trending shape; slow term disagreeing with the fade;
the signature sitting at or near POC rather than at a real extreme; the level
already retested twice and failed; a size-heavy push still producing yield; price
*accepted* at the extreme on the time-and-volume measure; or the highest-delta
print on the session profile contradicting the fade. Standing aside on a
disagreeing slow term is the highest-value abstention available, because that is
the exact population producing side_hit 0.36-0.57.

## Failure modes the sources name

- **Entering on the print itself.** Named as the most common mistake, ahead of the
  other three combined; it takes the quarter of absorptions that were never going
  to work. Guard: the reward system.
- **Chasing with a market order at the touch.** "You do what the losing aggressors
  do: pay the spread to push into a wall, enter before the flush." PF 0.81 versus
  1.80. Guard: rest inside the level and expect the 18-tick excursion.
- **Fading every touch.** -0.285R per trade; only 42% hold. Guard: the graded slow
  term.
- **Trusting raw order flow with no context.** Flow features alone, AUC 0.54.
  Guard: memory and location carry the arbiter, flow only sharpens it.
- **Fading a trend repeatedly** until the account is gone. Guard: trending-shape
  hard abstain.
- **Taking an absorption print at POC.** Filtered out entirely regardless of how
  clean. Guard: the location gate in pattern 1.
- **Anchoring to current-day VAH/VAL**, a recalculating value rather than a level.
  Guard: prior session's closed edges only.
- **Reading depth as acceptance.** A deep excursion into a level is not
  acceptance; only time and volume traded there is. Guard: acceptance measured as
  time-and-volume-at-price.
- **Reading heavy buy delta at a high as bullish.** It is a flag for trapped
  positioning, not a signal in that direction.
- **Defaulting to the 80% traverse case.** Guard: the traverse counter sets the
  target.
- **Treating a squeeze and an origin-of-move re-entry as the same trade.** A
  squeeze never failed; an OFM re-entry follows a failed squeeze and requires the
  aggressor to return with *more* aggression than the first attempt.
- **Jumping straight to breakeven**, named as "probably the worst thing you can do
  for your EV." Relevant to the replay's stop policy, not to feature construction.
- **Position and day limits.** One position at a time; stop for the day at -4R,
  which `refill-effect` calls the single most powerful rule it found and worth
  roughly 14 points of pass rate. This belongs to the existing policy layer, not
  to the learner.

## The five most decisive signatures

Ranked by how much each should move the verdict that *this quiet extreme is the
terminal one and the market is leaving it*.

1. **Exhaustion print into a finished auction, then a delta flip within one to two
   minutes.** Top-percentile `|delta|` into the extreme, bottom-percentile
   `yield_s`, `twoside` false at the extreme tick, and `delta` reversing sign in
   the next one or two minutes without a new extreme. This is the only signature
   in the library with an explicit timing constraint attached ("the turn is
   usually one or two candles away"), it is decoded with real numbers
   (-674 then +332 the next minute), and it is native to one-minute arithmetic.
   The `twoside` polarity is the part to test rather than assume.
2. **Effort with no result at the extreme.** Top-quartile attack volume against
   bottom-quartile adverse yield, with reloads above the session norm, and where
   an open/close is available the candle-versus-delta disagreement. The concrete
   picture is roughly 560 contracts of aggressive selling dumped into a 22-tick
   band in about 90 seconds, price yielding about 10 ticks and then stopping. This
   reads side directly, which is what the detector lacks.
3. **The level-owner counter.** The same side has won this price band at least
   twice, and the band's accumulated delta is signed that way. This is the only
   one of the five computed *away* from the extreme, and it is the one with
   independent measured support: flow alone AUC 0.54 against 0.63 with memory and
   location, and -0.285R against +0.143R. It is the direct fix for side_hit.
4. **Arrival mode into the extreme.** Fast and size-heavy, fast and count-heavy,
   or slow and grinding — computed from `pace`, `conc` and `churn` over the
   approach. `whos-in-control` applies this before any other read, and it
   separates a crowd that will be trapped from initiative that is not finished
   from disinterest that will simply break the level. The mapping's sign should be
   fit, since the source states it ambiguously.
5. **Control transfer after the quiet.** `delta` flips sign with `vol` at or above
   the session median and `quote_events` elevated, inside the confirm window, and
   price does not return to the extreme. Opposite-side prints of comparable size
   to the ones that made the extreme are the qualifying form — in the worked
   example, sell prints of 122/202/233 into the low answered by buy prints of
   186/253/262/217 carrying price 40-plus points. Last only because it arrives
   latest: it confirms rather than predicts, and the delay budget is what makes
   paying for it affordable.

The abstain counterpart is worth as much as any of the five: no balance anywhere
in the session — range expanding near-monotonically, `twoside` rarely set, no band
accumulating repeated two-sided visits.

## What is genuinely new versus the crosswalk

`amt-on-live-markets` and `reading-the-volume-profile` largely restate what
`disc_auction_` implements: balance and imbalance, fair value as a range, value
area edges, acceptance and rejection, high and low volume nodes, the failed
auction. Read them as confirmation that the existing family is faithful. New:

1. the 80/20 traverse odds with POC break-and-hold as the discriminator;
2. the five-shape taxonomy, with trending as an explicit abstain rather than a
   context flag;
3. P and B shapes as a directional prior for the slow term;
4. depth-is-not-acceptance, stated as a correction with a live example.

`a-clean-continuation-short` is the most useful of the three because it is a
worked session rather than a framework:

5. the higher-timeframe filter as a **hard gate** on every entry;
6. the refill zone defined by repetition, with a stop-out explicitly not
   disqualifying re-entry;
7. squeeze versus origin-of-move, with "more aggression than the first attempt" as
   the re-entry confirmation;
8. size-scaled prints defining the level, and the volume-delta versus
   count-delta split as separate witnesses.

From the diagram re-reads of the priority set, additions that were not in the
crosswalk's ledger:

9. the reward system, with its 27% failure rate and the "never enter on the print"
   rule;
10. the finished-versus-unfinished auction test at the bar extreme, and its decoded
    polarity at the terminal turn;
11. the minute-scale POC flip, defined between consecutive candles;
12. absorption as candle-delta *disagreement*, with agreement explicitly excluded;
13. the POC-location filter and the current-day-VAH/VAL warning;
14. the tiering rule "one tell is interest, two is a trade";
15. `refill-effect`'s measured family ablation, which is the strongest external

Items 15 and 5 are the strongest additions to the decision procedure; items 9, 10

## Evidence: the three new PDFs

### `a-clean-continuation-short.pdf` (14 pages)

A worked short session on a Nikkei-like contract around 29,400-30,250, closing
+$870 across two trades, framed honestly against a two-and-a-half-week
break-even-to-losing stretch on the same account. One higher-timeframe thesis,
three lower-timeframe confirmations, all the same direction.

The five-minute thesis (p4) is a minor volume node used previously as resistance,
with negative delta stacking into it. The chart carries a purple composite volume
profile plotted horizontally against price and a magenta **delta profile at
price** beside it — delta localized to price rather than accumulated over time,
which is the representational point worth carrying. Grey level bands are drawn at
roughly 29,750-29,765 and 29,495-29,520.

Failed auctions at the top (p5) marks a dark red band at roughly 29,700-29,745
that price wicked into repeatedly without closing and holding above. The corner
readout is the detail that matters: `Σ Vol 3.47M`, `Δ Vol -4.1K`, `Σ NT 459K`,
`Δ NT 10K`. Volume delta and trade-count delta carry **opposite signs** on the
same session.

The yearly composite (p7) shows the composite profile visibly pinched between
roughly 29,750 and 30,050 — the low-participation band price sliced through — and
bulging at 29,600-29,700. The refill zone is a grey band at 29,690-29,715 with a

The re-entry (p8) is a real order ticket on a one-minute chart: `SELL 1 | R:R
6.11`, stop `-180.00$ | 9.00` immediately above entry near 29,686, target

fifty-five of reward. Aggressor bubbles scale with print size; a very large one
lands at 09:28-09:30 into the red zone at 29,700, price fails to hold above, and
entry is taken on the retest five to seven minutes later. The first attempt at

Origin of the move retaken (p10) draws the `OFM` line at roughly 29,658 where the

### `amt-on-live-markets.pdf` (14 pages)

Fair value is a range, not a price. Extremes force exactly one question — does the
market want to keep accepting prices out here, or reject them and return to fair
value — and the reframe is to watch what the auction did after reaching the level
rather than deciding to buy or sell at it.

The failed-auction diagram (p8) is hand-drawn: price zigzags along a horizontal
balance line for three peaks, breaks down, plunges to a lower line marking
previous fair value, touches it once, and V-reverses straight back up. The failure
is visible as the *absence of structure* at the lower line — one spike, no time.

The 80/20 page (p9) assigns roughly 80% to a full trip to the far extreme and 20%
to ranging, once price is back in range after the failed auction, and names POC
behaviour as the tool that tells you which before it resolves.

The first live example (p10) is the depth-is-not-acceptance case: price dipped
deep enough into the previous balance to look accepted, still failed to hold and
trade there, and buyers eventually pushed it all the way back up.

The week-long balance (p11) is a light-themed NQ chart carrying three volume
profiles rendered with **red and blue bars — a delta-coloured profile** showing
which side dominated at each price, not merely how much traded there. The toolbar
carries `put wall`, `call wall`, `GEX flip` and `vol trigger` buttons, confirming
the author works with options positioning levels the crosswalk correctly records
as unavailable to us.

### `reading-the-volume-profile.pdf` (13 pages)

Theory only, part one of two. POC is described as a magnet within the balance that
price returns to regardless of approach direction, and its behaviour picks the
target: repeated failure to break above implies a trip to VAL, an aggressive push
through implies VAH.

The author states plainly that he trades continuations — break, retest, continue —
and rarely reversals, because continuations give him a higher win rate. Worth
carrying because our detector is nominally a reversal. The reconciliation is in the
case study: what he calls continuation *is* a fade of a local extreme taken in the
direction of the slower thesis.

The five shapes are covered in pattern 8. The trending page is the one to weight:
a narrow spread with no bell, continuously stair-stepping price, captioned with
the instruction to get off.

## Fidelity limits carried forward

The MBP-1 limits in the crosswalk are unchanged and are not silently filled by
anything above. Replenishment remains an execution proxy rather than order-ID
truth, so `reload_*` and `churn` support an iceberg *reading*, never an iceberg
identification. Direct options positioning is absent and the `GEX flip` and wall
levels visible in the source charts have no counterpart in our data. The POC
patterns require volume binned by price, and the candle-delta disagreement
requires a per-minute open and close; both degrade explicitly and should be
labelled as proxies if those inputs are not cached. Two of the priority documents
contain no footprint grids at all, so any claim resting on per-price bid-ask
numbers traces to `fp-lesson-9` and `trapped-buyers-one-retest` only.

None of the above is evidence that any of it pays in this house, on these assets,
at this grain. It is a specification for what to compute and how to combine it,
and every threshold is a starting percentile to be moved by measurement.
